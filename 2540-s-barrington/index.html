Buyer Profile & Transaction Strategy
Who Is Most Likely to Buy, and How We Create Competition

Market Context

Fifteen City of Los Angeles apartment sales of 8 to 14 units, built 1953 to 1973, closed between September 2024 and August 2026 at a median of $258,333 per unit, $341 per SF and 11.30 times rent. The closest match, 11296 Brookhaven Avenue, 12 units built in 1958 half a mile away, sold for $3.1M in September 2025 at 11.56 times rent, with the seller's low-rate loan taken over by the buyer. 1519 Armacost Avenue, 11 small units, closed in July 2026 at 10.48 times rent and a 5.89% cap.

Thirty days before each of those sales closed, the 5-year Treasury ran 3.61% to 4.40%, a median of 3.91%. On 09/22/2026 it stood at 4.83% (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, FRED series DGS5). The four closings from June to August 2026, which locked nearest today's rates, ran 9.16 to 11.19 times rent.

A buyer today pays about a point more for money than the buyers who set most of those comps, so the 2026 closings carry more weight in our pricing than the 2024 and 2025 sales.

Positioning Thesis

The right buyer wants a Westside pool building near two rail stations, four legacy tenancies to turn over time and an R4 lot to hold. We would qualify buyers early on their financing, because at today's rates the loan decides what a buyer can pay.

Likely Buyer Profiles

Likely Buyer 01

1031 Exchange Buyer

Exchange buyers who need a replacement property of this size on a 45-day identification clock. A 12-unit building with a documented rent roll is simple to underwrite on a deadline.

Likely Buyer 02

Westside Apartment Owner

Owners who already hold apartments in West Los Angeles, Palms and Mar Vista, including the buyers of the sales in this analysis. They know the tenant base and buy on rent upside and price per unit.

Likely Buyer 03

Long-Term Holder

Buyers who hold for decades and value an R4-1 lot near the E Line, pricing today's income while the zoning stays in reserve.

Transaction Strategy

01

Price Each Building on Today's Rates

The sales in this analysis priced when the 5-year Treasury ran 3.4% to 4.4% at lock; it stood at 4.83% on 09/22/2026. Each building is priced on its own income at a yield today's buyer can finance, so offers arrive in the first weeks rather than after a price reduction.

02

Two Buildings, Two Separate Sales

We recommend selling the two buildings separately, each at its own price, so each goes to the buyer who pays the most for it. Both launch in the same campaign, which reaches West Los Angeles and Mar Vista buyers at once.

The LAAA Team of Marcus & Millichap is pleased to present 2540 S Barrington Avenue, a 12-unit apartment building in West Los Angeles. The two-story wood frame building was completed in 1958 and holds ten one bedroom and two two bedroom units, 6,535 square feet on an 8,257 square foot R4-1 lot. The units open onto a courtyard pool. The building is registered under the Los Angeles Rent Stabilization Ordinance.

The investment case is rent growth on turnover in a building with one ownership since 1978. Scheduled rent of $251,328 grows to $292,800 at market, about 16.5% upside. Four tenancies that began between 1999 and 2018 pay $1,170 to $1,352, and the two units open on the August 2026 rent roll can be leased at market now.

The block is 0.7 miles from the Expo/Sepulveda E Line station, and the R4-1 lot allows about 20 units under base zoning against 12 today, a long-term option for a patient owner. The offering fits 1031 exchange buyers and West Los Angeles apartment owners who hold for the long term.

Location Overview
Sawtelle, West Los Angeles

The property sits on Barrington Avenue just south of Gateway Boulevard in the Sawtelle pocket of West Los Angeles, about 0.3 miles south of Pico Boulevard. Whole Foods Market on National Boulevard is 0.47 miles southeast and Marukai Market on Pico 0.49 miles west. Walk Score rates the block 76, carried by the restaurants at the Barrington and Gateway corner.

The Expo/Sepulveda station on the Metro E Line is 0.7 miles northeast, putting Santa Monica and Downtown Los Angeles on one rail line. The I-10 is about 570 feet south. Sony Pictures Studios is 2.49 miles east, Westfield Century City 2.51 miles northeast, UCLA 2.82 miles north and Santa Monica Place 3.09 miles west.

ZIP 90064 is 51% renter occupied, with a median household income of $135,383 and a median gross rent of $2,855 (2024 American Community Survey). Of its 12,954 housing units, 6,064 were built before 1960. The Exposition Corridor Transit Neighborhood Plan rezoned about 256 acres along the E Line for about 6,000 additional homes by 2035.
