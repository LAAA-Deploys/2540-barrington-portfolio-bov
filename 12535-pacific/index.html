Buyer Profile & Transaction Strategy
Who Is Most Likely to Buy, and How We Create Competition

Market Context

Nine City of Los Angeles apartment sales of 6 to 10 units, built 1954 to 1969, closed between September 2024 and April 2026 at a median of $261,111 per unit, $341 per SF and 11.52 times rent. 12610 Caswell Avenue, nine units built in 1964 two tenths of a mile away, sold for $2.395M in September 2024 at 11.33 times rent. 12757 Caswell Avenue, nine units with a similar mix of singles and two bedroom two baths, closed in March 2026 at $2.05M, 9.79 times rent and a 6.47% cap.

Thirty days before each of those sales closed, the 5-year Treasury ran 3.51% to 4.05%, a median of 3.68%. On 09/22/2026 it stood at 4.83% (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, FRED series DGS5).

A buyer today pays about a point more for money than the buyers who set most of those comps, so the 2026 sale at 12757 Caswell carries more weight in our pricing than the 2024 and 2025 sales.

Positioning Thesis

The right buyer wants a finished soft-story retrofit, recent leases that prove the rents and a walkable block off Venice Boulevard. We would qualify buyers early on their financing, because at today's rates the loan decides what a buyer can pay.

Owners who already hold apartments in Mar Vista and Palms, including the buyers of the sales in this analysis. They know the street and buy on rent upside and price per unit.

Buyers moving up from a duplex or fourplex who want nine units without a seismic project ahead of them.

Transaction Strategy

01

Price Each Building on Today's Rates

The sales in this analysis priced when the 5-year Treasury ran 3.5% to 4.4% at lock; it stood at 4.83% on 09/22/2026. Each building is priced on its own income at a yield today's buyer can finance, so offers arrive in the first weeks rather than after a price reduction.

02

Two Buildings, Two Separate Sales

We recommend selling the two buildings separately, each at its own price, so each goes to the buyer who pays the most for it. Both launch in the same campaign, which reaches West Los Angeles and Mar Vista buyers at once.

The LAAA Team of Marcus & Millichap is pleased to present 12535 Pacific Avenue, a 9-unit apartment building in Mar Vista. The two-story wood frame building was completed in 1962 and holds one single, four one bedroom and four two bedroom two bath units, 6,404 square feet on a 6,992 square foot R3-1 lot. Parking is tuck-under off the rear alley. The building is registered under the Los Angeles Rent Stabilization Ordinance.

The investment case is rent growth on turnover with the seismic work already done. Scheduled rent of $199,872 grows to $256,200 at market, about 28.2% upside, and four tenancies that began between 1997 and 2014 pay $812 to $1,484. The soft-story retrofit was certified in 2018 and the windows were replaced in 2013.

Five of the nine units have been re-leased since 2024, most recently a two bedroom two bath at $2,750 in September 2026, so the rent roll shows what the units command today. The offering fits 1031 exchange buyers and Westside owners who want a finished retrofit on a walkable Mar Vista block.

Location Overview
Mar Vista, a Block off Venice Boulevard

The property sits on Pacific Avenue a block south of Venice Boulevard in the center of Mar Vista, on a street of 1950s and 1960s apartment buildings. Grand View Boulevard Elementary is 0.35 miles east, Vons on Centinela Avenue 0.42 miles southeast and the Mar Vista Branch Library 0.47 miles northeast. Walk Score and Bike Score both rate the block 88.

The offices at Runway at Playa Vista are 2.1 miles southeast and Google's Spruce Goose campus 2.29 miles. Sony Pictures Studios is 2.27 miles northeast, Abbot Kinney Boulevard 1.99 miles west and Los Angeles International Airport 4.5 miles southeast. The I-405 and the Marina Freeway are about 1.2 miles away, and the Venice Boulevard bus corridor runs a block north.

ZIP 90066 is 62% renter occupied, with a median household income of $114,141 and a median home value of $1,695,400 (2024 American Community Survey). Small buildings are the core of its rental stock: 8,243 of its 27,045 housing units sit in buildings of 5 to 19 units. The city's Housing Element rezoning, effective 02/11/2025, mapped new housing sites along the Venice Boulevard corridor.
